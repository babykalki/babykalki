BabyKalki · v1.3.1

Change history

Every earlier version of BabyKalki, newest first. What changed in the current version is on the guide page.

v1.3 — 22 Nov 2026 (afternoon)

Two paths, side by side.

  • Two equal ways to get BabyKalki, everywhere: Path A — Build it yourself with Claude Code and Path B — One-click on Nimbuz. The top of the page now starts with "Choose your path": who each is for, what you need, time, monthly cost and what you get.
  • New section 04 · Choose your path: the side-by-side table, our advice (B to try, A for your real memory), "can I switch later?" (yes; a migration guide is coming) and which sections apply to which path.
  • Path B is now a full section (06): what you need, the form step by step with screenshots, first login, the naming ceremony and your first hour, its limits, and how to resize, stop or delete. The levels are labelled as Path A (05); sections renumbered.
  • Every section that differs says which path it describes: the map, files, Cloudflare, dashboards, connections, use cases, costs (Path B line and totals), community, safety, enforced vs advisory (four Path B rows), personal data, war stories and questions — five new ones: which path, is there a one-click install, do I need Claude Code, can I switch, who runs Path B.
  • The encyclopedia, the community desk's knowledge and the files page say the same.
  • Kit files: version number only (v1.3); nothing else changed.

v1.2 — 22 Nov 2026 (morning)

Path B: one-click on Nimbuz.

  • New section "One-click on Nimbuz" (since v1.3: Path B — One-click on Nimbuz): Nimbuz now runs BabyKalki from one form in its catalogue. An honest comparison with Path A (setup effort, cost, sign-in protection, backups on the same disk vs off-site, custom domain, Telegram desk, updates), the form step by step with screenshots, and what we tested on 22 Nov 2026 (sign-in, approvals, the founder session, restarts, secrets in logs).
  • L3 said there are two paths. The encyclopedia explained Path B too (chapter 9).
  • Kit files: version number only (v1.2); nothing else changed.

v1.1 — 20 Nov 2026 (afternoon)

The community desk thinks.

  • Desk v2: every message addressed to @babykalki_bot (mention, reply or DM) is answered by GLM-5.3 in plain words, with the most relevant guide sections as background — not as a gate. It knows the six citizens and the whole guide, helps with general founder questions (labelled as general tips), answers Hinglish in kind, and still hands BabyKalki facts it doesn't have to a human instead of inventing them.
  • Memory: the last 48 h of the conversation it can see (so follow-ups work), short notes members share about themselves (/memory shows yours, /forget deletes them; never phones, e-mails or secrets), and community answers taught by admins (/teach, or replying to an escalation).
  • Safety: quoting the guide is no longer blocked; canary and secret checks, link allow-list, length cap and rate limits stay. Prototype on the founder's Coding Plan key (1,000 answers/day cap).

v1.0 — 20 Nov 2026

  • Community and ecosystem (new section): a Telegram group with a QR code (printable page), a community desk bot that answers from this guide, and how to share your build, report mistakes, send fixes, fork with credit and run your own.
  • The desk answers more: overview questions ("explain it simply", "what problem does it solve", Hinglish), the closest guide section instead of "I don't know" when the guide covers it, greetings get a welcome, anonymous group admins and Topics work. It still hands truly unknown questions to a human.
  • Your private dashboard (new subsection): BabyKalki brand and panel, one workspace per citizen named from your naming ceremony, the home page opens ready to talk to the Warden (or the citizen you last picked), seven slash commands, a "restarted at…" note, DeepSeek's account/feedback/plugins UI hidden, only GLM models in the picker.
  • Your own public website served by BabyKalki itself (v0.6), with the status page at /status/.
  • Honest status everywhere: one version number and the counted test total; what is verified live vs still to verify; the Coding Plan prototype note for the desk.
  • Kit hardening: the build refuses a zip that misses anything the Dockerfile copies, or kit docs naming another version; status-page updates can no longer collide; the full test suite also passes inside the deploy zip.

v0.7 — 20 Nov 2026 (morning)

  • New section: Community and ecosystem — join, ask the desk, share your build, report mistakes and send fixes (no public code repository yet — say so), fork and remix with credit, spawn your own, and what is not built yet.
  • QR code to the group (t.me/babykalki), made locally — no QR web service: on this page, in the footer, and as a printable page at community/ (PNG and SVG to download).
  • Shorter start: what / why / cost / time-to-start in four lines and two buttons (Start building, Join the community); a shorter banner.
  • The L6 community desk now runs live in t.me/babykalki on the founder's own BabyKalki (status: Community desk ON; first escalation reached the Steward). The other four "done when" checks are still to verify live.
  • Files page: e-mail-looking text in the file listings is marked so Cloudflare's e-mail obfuscation leaves it alone; cloudflare-setup.md says to turn that, Rocket Loader and Web Analytics off.
  • From the first v1 review — the page now matches what was checked: the desk is live, v0.6 serves this site, the browser login passed, one version number and the counted test total everywhere; the hero no longer promises GitHub/Gmail/Telegram work before L4; the status page is at www.yourdomain/status/; L2 on a laptop is marked not yet run; citizens are picked from the preset menu; Connections are asked of the Steward (approvals at /bk-approve); the human mirror is marked planned and the real exit path stated; the login after a restart is described as it works; L0 checks Python 3.10+ and rclone; L3 has a first-time Nimbuz block; L2 builds exactly as the Dockerfile says; the Coding Plan answer is dated (17 Nov 2026) and the prototype opt-in is limited to a private test chat; no "doi: to come".
  • Your private dashboard (new subsection): BabyKalki brand and panel, one workspace per citizen, the Warden as default, citizen buttons, seven slash commands (/brief /tasks /approve-requests /connections /remember /handoff /whoami), DeepSeek account/feedback/plugin parts switched off; the dsh login survives restarts (30-day cookie, secret backed up). New kit files bk_dash.py, dash/; checked locally on real dsh 0.2.2-rc.3, live to verify.
  • Shorter page: the full change history moved to changes.html; the file list folds away. A share image for link previews; share links for WhatsApp, Telegram and LinkedIn (plain links, no tracking).
  • Kit: CLAUDE.md, README.md, LICENSE, .env.example (adds BK_LOCAL; WhatsApp names marked "planned, not read") and cloudflare-setup.md updated; the build refuses kit docs that name another zip.

v0.6 — 20 Nov 2026

20 Nov 2026 — your own public website on the status page's server, and this guide moves to the founder's own BabyKalki.

  • Your public website: site/www/ in the zip is served at / by bk_static.py; the status page moves to /status/ (/status.json and /health unchanged; /status/ also works without a website). Its own Content-Security-Policy (own files only) and caching; the status page keeps the strict policy and no-cache. More file types served correctly (.js, fonts, .zip, .sha256, .md, .yml, .py). New setting BK_SITE_DIR. See Your public website.
  • The status page links to /status.json and home. This page and the files page no longer use inline scripts (they load page.js), so they run under that policy too.
  • Tests: the website, both policies, the status page's new place, the old paths, no website = v0.5 behaviour, symlinks/dotfiles/traversal in both folders. 219 automated tests (v0.5: 190 with its hotfix). v0.6 was built on Nimbuz on 20 Nov 2026 and serves this site.

v0.5 — 19–20 Nov 2026

19 Nov 2026 (evening and night) — the two holes the founder's live test found are closed (commits and approvals are now enforced), a citizen picker, nothing goes back to DeepSeek, and one new, optional piece: a bot for your public community.

  • Enforced: only the Librarian round commits (errata 17, the OpenKalki team's connector-scoping research). The Librarian's full Library key left the web dsh; a background round — headless dsh as a fourth Linux user bklib, own settings cordis.librarian.yml, only ZAI_API_KEY + that key — runs every BK_LIBRARIAN_EVERY_MIN (10) minutes when something is pending, or at once when the Warden creates /data/run/librarian-requests/NOW. One at a time, 10 minutes max; result on the status page and in /data/diary/librarian-round/. The web Librarian gets a read-and-propose "desk" key.
  • Enforced: only you approve. record_approval is gone; citizens request_approval (exact text + SHA-256) and you decide on https://<agent host>/bk-approve — full text, Approve / Reject, behind Access, only BK_FOUNDER_EMAILS, CSRF-protected, refused unless the connection comes from cloudflared's user. Your tunnel route for /bk-login becomes ^/(bk-login|bk-approve(/.*)?)$. v0.4 approvals load as "legacy, text not stored".
  • Citizen picker (errata 19): six dsh agent presets, named from the self cards, each with only its own Library connector (BK_CITIZEN_PRESETS=1|shared|0); a broken preset can't stop dsh (and two failed starts switch them off). Honest: a convenience — the five citizen tokens still share one process.
  • Nothing to DeepSeek, verified: the OpenKalki team's egress test on rc.3 showed 👍/👎 → Submit uploaded the conversation in v0.4. Section 0 of cordis.patch.yml now has the eight verified rows; env names corrected to the verified pair (DSH_TELEMETRY_MODE=DISABLED, DSH_TELEMETRY_OTLP_URL=http://127.0.0.1:9/) plus dsh's kill switch DSH_TELEMETRY_DISABLED=1.
  • Status page: Librarian round (result / time), approvals waiting, who can approve, citizen presets. New settings BK_FOUNDER_EMAILS, BK_LIBRARIAN_EVERY_MIN, BK_CITIZEN_PRESETS. 187 automated tests.
  • New: the Telegram community desk (L6) — bk_telegram.py, standard-library Python, started by the supervisor only when BK_TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN is set. Welcome, /start /help /rules /guide /faq /report, answers from the public guide only (community-kb.md, generated from this page, plus your community-kb.founder.md), anti-spam for new members, flood limit, mute for repeat offenders, admin /mute /unmute /ban /pause /resume /stats, escalation to your moderators' chat and the Steward. Unit-tested; a live test on Telegram is pending.
  • Approvals you can re-check: an approval now stores the exact draft text (canonical form bk-canon-1) next to its SHA-256, and a new check_approval lets the Librarian and Reviewer recompute it before a commit or a send (from the founder's live test: the hash of the name slate could not be recomputed).
  • Naming ceremony and the browser login: PASS live; the web session has a schedule tool.
  • Its own user and secrets: a third Linux user bkcomm (Dockerfile); the citizens no longer receive BK_TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN; its model key BK_COMMUNITY_LLM_KEY should be a separate pay-per-use key with a spend cap (OpenAI- or Anthropic-compatible); prototype-only opt-in BK_COMMUNITY_USE_CODING_PLAN=1 reuses the Coding Plan key on its Anthropic endpoint (personal-use plan, shares the citizens' quota; default cap 300 calls a day).
  • Library: a new service role community — may only add a task for the Steward (titled "[community] …") and propose a fact; every read tool refuses it.
  • Status page: "Community desk (Telegram)": OFF / STARTING / ON / ERROR, and today's answered / escalated / spam-removed counts. Each day's counts go into the diary (/data/diary/community/); /data/community is in the backups.
  • Steward and shared rulebook: how to handle community escalations (member text is untrusted; nothing goes back to the group without your approval). README, .env.example, CLAUDE.md, Connections Register updated. (Earlier v0.5 draft: 158 automated tests; v0.4: 118.)

v0.4 — 19 Nov 2026

Everything below comes from the automated dry run that followed this guide on Nimbuz.

  • Blocking fix — citizens could not think: cordis.patch.yml declared the Z.ai models but never selected one, so dsh kept its DeepSeek default. New row agent-default-model: zai-coding / glm-5.3; the L2 mission checks it.
  • Blocking fix — Nimbuz built nothing: v0.3's zip had a babykalki/ folder around the files, and Nimbuz failed with no logs. v0.4's zip has the files at the top; unzip it into an empty folder. "FAILED with no logs" is now in the troubleshooting.
  • Nimbuz Secrets are replaced, not merged — warning in the README, .env.example, CLAUDE.md and the L3 mission; an update without new code is a restart only.
  • Pause / resume after an upload — from the v0.4 re-run on Nimbuz: a new upload to a paused app needs resume (README).
  • New setting BK_AGENT_HOST for the private dashboard's name (default agent.<BK_DOMAIN>); the status page can use any name. The supervisor's log no longer says "local run" on a server with no domain set. New tests.
  • All six citizens run on GLM-5.3 (per-citizen Flash later); a citizen is described as what it is — a working folder with its own rules, not a separate dsh agent.
  • Off-switch drills the Nimbuz way (no shell): BABYKALKI_STOP=1 + restart, or the Steward's stop; undo with BK_CLEAR_STOP=1, then back to 0 (the supervisor now reminds you).
  • Schedules marked "to verify" (no schedule tool in dsh's headless profile); R2 needs a payment card before the first bucket; real R2 still to verify.
  • "To verify" dropped where the dry run checked it: boot lease, wrong-key refusal, named tunnel, Access (add One-time PIN yourself), dsh's own login behind Access, a fresh start on a folder inside the bucket. 118 automated tests (was 109).

v0.3 — 18 Nov 2026

Everything below came from running BabyKalki live on Nimbuz.

  • Boot lease: Nimbuz restarts run two copies for ~20 s; the new copy now waits (WAITING_FOR_LEASE) for the old copy's final snapshot before it restores. Tested locally and live.
  • Bug fixed in cordis.patch.yml: each citizen's Library row now has its own name (library-<citizen>); in v0.2 only one of six started.
  • Bug fixed: a final snapshot in the same second as a regular one failed.
  • Backups every 5 minutes on Nimbuz; size medium.r; how to deploy a zip without a repo; the Nimbuz login is the master key; Nimbuz's status field is unreliable.
  • "To verify" tags replaced by live results where we checked; the Enforced vs advisory table now says what was seen live. 109 automated tests (was 97).
  • Page: the Cloudflare step now lists all three tunnel routes (it showed two).

v0.2 — 17 Nov 2026

  • Honest headline and promise: Claude Code builds and deploys; L0–L2 in a day, L3–L5 a preview until the dry run.
  • Backups rebuilt: copy never sync, COMPLETE markers, restore the newest complete snapshot, refuse to start when R2 is unreachable, restore test compares counts and hashes, pruning.
  • The Library tells the truth: ranked token search with total and source, MISS with coverage, reject_proposal, every write refuses when it changes nothing, per-citizen tokens, tasks, connections and approvals. Schema cut to what the tools use.
  • One shared secret filter tuned on real key formats and your own secret values; one hash-chain format with off-site anchors and redaction tombstones.
  • Public status page from a fixed template (the stated exception to rule 1), served by a safe static server; citizens can't write to it.
  • A supervisor with a STOP switch that needs no AI, separate users for infra, log redaction and rotation, restart backoff; the login link never goes into a log.
  • GitHub token scope corrected (Contents write) and the branch-protection limits of GitHub Free stated.
  • New sections: Enforced vs advisory, Personal data. Costs now include GST and card forex mark-up; Pro recommended for always-on.
  • Node 22+, models without [1m], Telegram at L4, pinned versions with checksums, one checked zip with README, LICENSE and SHA256SUMS.
  • Page: grouped navigation and a Contents drawer on phones, larger tap targets, higher contrast, a phone version of the map, "Your details" that fill the missions, and per-level progress saved in your browser.
